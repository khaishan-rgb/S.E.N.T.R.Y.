Halfway or continue?

One bus runs late. Here are three ways to handle it, and what passengers at a stop see in the hour around the disruption (30 min before to 30 min after). Each option keeps one headway for every trip, so the numbers stay put.

Best for passengers

What each option does to the gaps between buses

Each picture is one hour at a stop. A triangle is one gap: the taller it is, the longer people wait. The dotted outline is the normal timetable. For halfway, trip 1 is one picture split at the halfway stop (◆): stops before it on the left, stops after it on the right, each side as wide as its share of the stops.

Why one long gap costs so much

  1. People turn up at random. Someone who arrives just after a bus waits the whole gap; someone who arrives just before waits almost nothing. On the picture, the height at any moment is how long a person arriving then will wait.
  2. The area of a triangle is the total waiting. Double the gap and the triangle holds four small ones: twice as many people arrive in it, and each waits twice as long.
  3. So spreading a gap always helps, and getting back to the timetable sooner helps most. Halfway closes the gap at the halfway stop and the timetable is back from the next trip. Adjustment spreads the gap over every trip. No adjustment keeps it for every trip.

How the numbers are worked out

Extra wait (EWT) = average wait in the hour minus the normal wait. Lower is better; 0 means passengers wait no longer than the timetable promises.
