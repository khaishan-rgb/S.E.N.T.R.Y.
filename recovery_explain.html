Halfway or continue?

One bus runs late. Here are three ways to handle it, and what passengers at a stop see in the hour around the disruption (30 min before to 30 min after). Each option keeps one headway for every trip, so the numbers stay put.

Best for passengers

What each option does to the gaps between buses

Each picture is one hour at a stop. A triangle is one gap: the taller it is, the longer people wait. The dotted outline is the normal timetable. For halfway, trip 1 is one picture split at the halfway stop (◆): stops before it on the left, stops after it on the right, each side as wide as its share of the stops.

Why one long gap hurts so much

Say one passenger reaches the stop every minute. In the picture each bar is one passenger, and its height is how long they wait. The first person after a bus waits the longest; the last one hardly waits.

    How the numbers are worked out

    Extra wait = how many minutes longer the average passenger waits than the timetable promises. 0 means no extra wait. Lower is better.
